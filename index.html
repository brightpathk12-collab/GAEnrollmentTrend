Your Enrollment Trend, FY2016 – FY2027

October (Count 1) drives QBE funding; March (Count 3) tells the end-of-year story. Toggle to overlay your size peers. FY27 shows the October count only; the March count has not been released yet.

Grade Bands and Fall-to-Spring Attrition

Grade bands (left) show which parts of the pipeline are growing or shrinking. Fall-to-spring (right) shows students lost (or gained) between the October and March counts each year.

Your Pipeline: Who Is Coming Up Behind Your Seniors

Enrollment in each grade this year (left), and the class entering against the class leaving over time (right). If your youngest classes are your smallest, a decline is already working its way up through the grades.

Mid-Stream: Are Families Joining or Leaving?

This follows the same classes from one October to the next: last year's 1st–7th graders against this year's 2nd–8th graders. Above zero means more students joined those classes than left. Kindergarten and high school are left out because retention and transfers distort them.

The State Picture, For Context

Georgia's October count fell to 1,672,651 in FY27, down 42,380 students (-2.5%) from FY26 — the largest one-year drop in this panel, ahead of FY20 → FY21 (-39,655).
Source: Georgia Department of Education, FTE Enrollment by Grade Level (PK-12) reports, FY2016-1 through FY2027-1. Twelve-year consolidated panel across all Georgia school systems.
